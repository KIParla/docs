= KIParla transcription conventions
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 2

KIParla transcriptions use the enriched notation system, adapted for spoken
Italian. This page is the *shared reference* that every KIParla module follows.

Individual modules may add symbols or narrow a rule for their own material.
Where they do, it is documented on that module's own *Transcription conventions*
page — see xref:#module-specific[Module-specific conventions] below.
Anything not listed on a module page follows this page unchanged.

toc::[]

[#module-specific]
== Module-specific conventions

Each module's documentation carries a *Transcription conventions* page with two
sections:

Extensions:: symbols or uses that this page does not define, added by the module
(for example a module-specific comment tag).
Overrides:: cases where the module applies a stricter or different rule than the
one stated here (for example a narrower timing range for `(.)`).

Anything a module page does not mention follows this page unchanged. Module
pages link back to the relevant section here by anchor (for example the
`#overlap` or `#pauses` fragment of this page's URL) — the section anchors are
stable, so keep them when editing.

[#principles]
== General principles

Transcribe what is said:: Write what the speaker actually produces, not a
corrected version. If a speaker says _le violenza_, transcribe `le violenza`.

Capitalisation:: Capital letters mark xref:#volume[higher volume] only. Do *not*
capitalise proper nouns, personal names, or place names.

Numbers:: Always write numbers out in letters (`due`, not `2`), so that
prolongations and overlaps can be transcribed on them. Arabic numerals are
converted to Italian words automatically during processing (`NUMBERS` rule).

Acronyms and initialisms:: Spell them out as they are *said* — the Italian
letter-names as separate words, digits as words: `elle due`, not `l due`,
`l 2`, or `LD`.

Never lengthen `h`:: A prolonged vowel is marked on the *vowel*, never on a
following `h`: write `e:::h`, not `eh:::` (see xref:#prolongation[Sound
prolongation]).

[#units]
== Transcription units and silence

A transcription unit begins and ends exactly when the speaker begins and ends
the utterance. If there is silence at the start or end of a unit, the boundary
is in the wrong place — move it, do not pad the unit with a pause.

Do not run consecutive units together. Except in rare cases, units are kept
apart using the (even very short) pauses that separate them.

`(.)` is only for short pauses *inside* a unit — see xref:#pauses[Pauses].

[#intonation]
== Intonation

Final punctuation on a token marks the intonation contour of that token. It is
*not* orthographic punctuation.

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Symbol | Contour

| `,` | Weakly rising
| `?` | Rising
| `.` | Falling
|===

A token with no final intonation mark carries level or unmarked intonation.

[#prolongation]
== Sound prolongation

`:` marks a stretched sound and is placed immediately after the prolonged vowel
or consonant (`ma:`, `bel:o`). Repeat the colon to show a longer stretch
(`ma::`); the number of colons is proportional to the duration.

Put the colon on the vowel, never on a following `h` (`e:::h`, not `eh:::`).
An xref:#overlap[overlap bracket] may fall in the middle of a prolongation:
`[e:]::h`.

[#linking]
== Prosodic linking (latching)

`=` joins two tokens that follow each other with no gap or pause between them.
It is written between the two linked tokens, which may belong to the same
speaker or to different speakers.

[#pauses]
== Pauses

`(.)` marks a short pause *within* a transcription unit, usually between 0.08
and 0.2 seconds.

* Never place `(.)` at the start or end of a unit — a leading or trailing pause
  is removed automatically (`TRIM_PAUSES` rule). Silence at a unit edge means
  the xref:#units[boundary] should move.
* `(.)` is transcribed literally as a token. Longer, timed pauses follow the
  conventions of the module's alignment layer rather than this notation.

[#volume]
== Volume

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Symbol | Meaning

| `TEXT` (uppercase)
| Louder than the surrounding speech. Applied to whole tokens. This is the
  *only* use of capital letters — not proper nouns or place names.

| `°TEXT°`
| Quieter than the surrounding speech. The degree signs enclose the affected
  span, which may be part of a token or several tokens.
|===

[#pace]
== Pace

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Symbol | Meaning

| `>TEXT<`
| The enclosed span is spoken faster than the surrounding speech.

| `<TEXT>`
| The enclosed span is spoken slower than the surrounding speech.
|===

[#interrupted]
== Interrupted tokens

A trailing `~` marks a token that is cut off (`vole~`), typically a false start
or self-repair. The `~` attaches directly to the truncated token with no space.

[#overlap]
== Overlapping speech

`[` and `]` enclose the stretch of a token or tokens that overlaps with another
speaker. The overlapping speakers' bracketed spans are aligned in the
transcription so that the start of each `[` marks the same point in time.

Use square brackets when *two speakers speak at the same time* — this is the
important case and must always be marked.

Do *not* use square brackets when:

* one speaker is talking and another only laughs (or produces other non-verbal
  behaviour);
* speech runs over background music.

Brackets may fall inside a token (`s[cherzavo]`) and may even split a
xref:#prolongation[prolongation] (`[e:]::h`). They never split a non-verbal
behaviour or a short pause: `((ri[de))]` is not valid — an `((...))` span or a
`(.)` is either wholly inside or wholly outside an overlap.

[#uncertain]
== Uncertain and unintelligible speech

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Symbol | Meaning

| `(TEXT)`
| The transcriber's best guess at speech that is hard to make out. The round
  brackets enclose the uncertain span.

| `xxx`
| Speech that cannot be made out at all. The number of `x`s roughly matches the
  number of syllables heard.
|===

[#reduced]
== Phonetically reduced forms

Round brackets *inside or touching a word* mark sounds that belong to the
canonical form of the word but were not pronounced — a phonetic reduction, not
transcriber uncertainty. The letters outside the brackets are what was heard;
the letters inside are the reduced-away part.

[cols="1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Written | Canonical form | Heard as

| `c(io)è` | cioè | "cè"
| `sc(u)ola` | scuola | "scola"
| `m(e l)o` | me lo | "melo" — a reduction spanning a word boundary
|===

A reduced form is resolved to its canonical spelling and the token(s) it covers
are marked `Reduced=Yes` in the derived data. This only happens when the
reconstructed word or phrase is on the module's *reduction whitelist* — see
`reduction_words` in the module's
https://github.com/KIParla/tools/blob/main/configs/defaults.yml[pipeline
configuration]. A bracketed word-internal span whose reconstruction is *not*
whitelisted is treated as an ordinary xref:#uncertain[uncertain span] instead.

The whitelist is module-configurable: a module that reduces forms the shared
list does not cover records them on its own *Transcription conventions* page and
adds them to its config.

[#nvb]
== Non-verbal behaviour, noise and music

`(( ))` encloses transcriber comments and non-verbal behaviour — laughter,
sighs, coughs, gestures relevant to the interaction, and similar
(`((ride))`, `((si schiarisce la voce))`). The text inside is a description, not
a transcription, and is written in Italian.

Reading and singing:: At the end of every unit that is read aloud or sung, add
`((legge))` or `((canta))`.

Background noise:: Transcribe a background noise only when it is relevant to the
interaction. Routine ambient noise is left out.

The `environment` tier:: Everything that is not a participant's turn is *not* put in the
speech tiers. It goes on one dedicated tier named `environment`:
* background noise and sounds, one entry per event (`((televisione))`);
* music, jingles and inserts, one entry per insert (`((sigla))`, `((canzone))`);
* actions of a whole group (`((guardano il video))`);
* speech that comes from an external source — a video, the television, the radio, a
  recording played during the conversation. It is transcribed like any other speech
  (including `#_` when it is not in Italian). When it is not in Italian, add its Italian
  translation on a child tier `environment_trad`, like the `<speaker>_trad` tiers of the
  speakers.
+
`environment` is not a participant: it is not listed in the metadata of the conversation.

[#language]
== Language variation and code-switching

Two markers record that speech is in a code other than standard Italian.

`#` before a single token::
Marks a token that is *certainly not Italian*. The test is the dictionary
(e.g. https://dizionario.internazionale.it/[dizionario.internazionale.it]);
because geographic synonyms (_geosinonimi_) are in the dictionary, they count
as Italian and are *not* marked. If you are in doubt — for instance a form
close to an Italian word — do *not* mark it.

`#_` at the start of a unit::
Marks that the *whole unit* is in a code other than Italian.

In the derived data a `#` token gets `Variation=Token` and a `#_` unit is
tagged as language variation on every token. The `#` marker set is
module-configurable — some modules disable it or normalise its position; check
the module's page.

[#interjections]
== Interjections, disfluencies and discourse markers

Interjections, backchannels, filled pauses and discourse markers are transcribed
with a *fixed canonical spelling* so the same vocalisation is always written the
same way. Use the form in the left column; do not use the variants in the right
column. The listed variants are corrected automatically during processing (the
`WORD_CORRECTIONS` normalization rule; see the module
https://github.com/KIParla/tools/blob/main/configs/defaults.yml[pipeline
configuration]).

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Canonical form | Do not write

| `cioè` (or the reduced form `c(io)è`)
| `cè`, `ceh`

| `mah`
| `mha`

| `ehm`
| `emh`, `hem`

| `eh`
| `he`

| `mh`
| `m`, `hm`

| `mhmh`
| `mmh`

| `hi`
| `ih`

| `vabbè`
| `vabbe`, `va beh`, `va be'`, `va be`
|===

Never lengthen `h` (see xref:#prolongation[Sound prolongation]).

Like the reduction whitelist, this list is module-configurable — a module may
extend or replace it and documents any change on its own *Transcription
conventions* page.
